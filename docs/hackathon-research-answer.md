We looked at research on rehab needs, sticking with physiotherapy, and remote care. Based on 2019 global estimates, the [WHO reports](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/musculoskeletal-conditions) that 1.71 billion people live with musculoskeletal conditions and estimates that [2.4 billion](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/rehabilitation) could benefit from rehabilitation. Reviews have identified barriers to keeping up with outpatient physiotherapy ([Jack et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.math.2009.12.004)) and found real-time telerehabilitation can achieve results comparable to standard care for musculoskeletal conditions ([Cottrell et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1177/0269215516645148)). That’s why PhysioCare is exploring simple movement feedback and progress tracking between appointments.

For Hong Kong context, a 2024 survey reported that 76.3% of people aged 65 and over preferred to age at home, while 3.6% had used community elderly-care services in the previous year ([Census and Statistics Department, 2026](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202607/31/P2026073100370.htm)). It doesn’t measure physiotherapy access or home-exercise habits, so we see it as context—not proof of local demand or product impact. We’ll need to validate those questions with patients and therapists here.
